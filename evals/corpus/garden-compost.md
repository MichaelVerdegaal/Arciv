# Composting notes

## Browns and greens

Aim for roughly two parts carbon-rich browns (dry leaves, cardboard, straw)
to one part nitrogen-rich greens (vegetable peels, grass clippings, coffee
grounds). A pile that smells of ammonia has too many greens; one that sits
cold and inert needs more.

## Turning and moisture

Turn the pile weekly once it heats up — steam on a cold morning means the
thermophilic phase is working. Squeeze test: a handful should feel like a
wrung-out sponge. Cover during long rain, water during drought.

## What to keep out

No meat, dairy, or oily leftovers in an open pile; they draw rats. Citrus
and onion are fine in moderation despite the folklore. Weed seeds survive a
cool pile, so hot-compost or bin them separately.
